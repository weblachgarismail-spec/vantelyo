App Permissions: Camera and Notifications

What Are App Permissions?

App permissions are the access rights an app requests to use your device's features — camera, microphone, location, contacts, notifications, and more.

The Problem with Upfront Permissions

Many apps ask for all permissions at installation time, before you've even used them. This is a privacy red flag. A calculator app asking for your contacts at install is unnecessary and suspicious.

Just-in-Time Permissions

Good apps ask for permissions only when they need them:

  • A camera app asks for camera access when you tap the photo button
  • A navigation app asks for location when you start navigation
  • A contacts app asks for contacts when you open your address book

How FreshGuard Handles Permissions

FreshGuard uses just-in-time permissions:

  • Camera: Only requested when you tap the scan button to read a barcode
  • Notifications: Only enabled when you save an item with an expiry date — and you can turn them off anytime
  • No other permissions: FreshGuard never asks for location, contacts, or microphone

How to Review Permissions on Your Phone

Android:

  • Settings → Apps → [App Name] → Permissions
  • Or: Settings → Privacy → Permission Manager

iOS:

  • Settings → [App Name] → review permissions
  • Or: Settings → Privacy & Security → [Permission Type]

Take Action

Review the permissions on your apps today. Revoke any that don't make sense. Choose apps like FreshGuard that ask for permissions only when needed.

Just-in-time permissions are the safest default: an app should ask for the camera only when you scan a barcode, and for location only when a feature genuinely needs it. Be cautious with apps that request access before you understand why. If a permission does not match the app core function, deny it and look for an alternative.

Practical checklist

  • Review permissions once a quarter.
  • Prefer "while using the app" for location access.
  • Revoke camera, microphone, and contacts access when unused.
  • Turn off ad personalization if you prefer non-targeted ads.
  • Keep your device encrypted and back up important data.

Our position

Vantelyo does not require accounts for its apps, does not sell personal data, and designs core features to work offline. Where advertising or analytics could be introduced, we explain it in the privacy policy and ask for consent first.
